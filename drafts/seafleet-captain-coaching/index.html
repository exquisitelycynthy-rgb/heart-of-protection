Why this matters

The hardest part of command often happens in the last few cables.

Most incidents in confined waters don't come from a lack of knowledge. They come from small judgement calls made under time pressure, with incomplete information and a bridge team that isn't quite in sync.

Simulator courses help, but they rarely reflect your ship's handling, your trading pattern or the way you make decisions. That gap is what this programme closes.

  • New command, unfamiliar hullDifferent windage, pivot point and power response from your last vessel, and no time to learn it gently.
  • Marginal weather decisionsKnowing exactly when to go, wait, or abort, and being able to defend that call afterwards.
  • Master–pilot dynamicsKeeping situational authority without undermining the pilot or the team.
  • Scrutiny after near-missesVetting, PSC and internal audits that expect evidence of competence, not just certificates.

Questions

Before you book.

Is this confidential?

Yes. What you share in the assessment and coaching stays between you and your coach. If your company sponsors the programme, we agree in advance exactly what is reported back.

Can you coach while I'm at sea?

Yes. Most of the programme works remotely around your watch schedule. On-board sessions are arranged where port calls and company approval allow.

Does this replace simulator or statutory training?

No. It complements them. Statutory courses give you the qualification; coaching helps you apply it to your actual vessel and trade.

Can my company pay for it?

Often, yes. We provide a programme outline and written records that support training budgets and SMS documentation.

What happens on the Bridge Assessment call?

We talk through your background, vessel and the manoeuvres you want to improve. You'll leave with at least one practical takeaway, and an honest view of whether the programme is worth it for you.
